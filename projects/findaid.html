FindAid

A web app built in one hackathon weekend that helps students, especially those from low-income backgrounds, find colleges they can afford and keep track of their options.

01

Why it matters

58%

of students say financial aid info is hard to find

1 in 3

students skip applying to schools they assume they can't afford

40%

of Pell Grant-eligible students never enroll in college

The hackathon challenge was to build something for social impact. Our team kept coming back to college costs. Tuition has gone up 169% since 1980, 43.5 million Americans have student loan debt, and the average borrower owes over $37,000. A lot of students rule out schools before they ever see what they'd actually pay after aid.

How might we

How might we make financial aid information simple enough that cost doesn't stop students from applying?

02

The solution

FindAid shows students the real cost of college, one school at a time.

Each college card shows the location, type, and length of the program, plus the numbers that matter most: acceptance rate, average cost after aid, the share of students receiving federal loans and Pell Grants, and median debt after graduation. Students can save schools they like or skip the ones they don't, and their picks show up in a saved list.

03

My role

It was my first hackathon. I designed the FindAid logo in Canva, a magnifying glass over a graduation cap, to show the idea of searching for an education. I also made the site's favicon and got it live, and I prototyped the front end, and then our team built the site with React and Python Flask, pulling real data from the College Scorecard API.
